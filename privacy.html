Privacy Policy for PhotoVault

Effective Date: October 7, 2026 • Application: PhotoVault (Android) • Developer: Rohit Kumar

1. Overview & Commitment to Privacy

PhotoVault is a local-first personal media organizer and backup application designed to give you complete ownership and privacy over your photos and videos.

PhotoVault is architected with a strict zero-telemetry, zero-intermediary-server principle. We do not host central databases, user tracking services, or cloud intermediary relays. All synchronization and cloud backup operations occur directly and securely between your personal Android device and your authenticated Google Drive storage accounts via official Google APIs.

2. Google API Services User Data Policy & Limited Use Disclosure

Google API Limited Use Compliance Statement

PhotoVault's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Specifically:

  • No External Server Transfers: Google user data is accessed solely on your client device and transferred directly to Google Drive endpoints. It is never transmitted to, collected by, or stored on external servers or third parties.
  • No Advertising or Marketing: Google user data is never used or transferred for serving advertisements, retargeting, personalized advertising, or data brokerage.
  • No AI Model Training: Google user data and your private media are never used to train generalized artificial intelligence (AI) or machine learning (ML) models.
  • No Human Inspection: No human (including the developer) has access to or reads your private photos, videos, or Google Drive files, unless you explicitly share debug logs that you have personally inspected and approved.

3. Google Drive Permissions & Scopes Requested

To perform backup, cloud pooling, and media synchronization, PhotoVault requests the following specific OAuth 2.0 scopes from Google:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file (Create & Manage App Files):
    Allows PhotoVault to create, upload, retrieve, organize, and delete files and folders that were created by or explicitly opened with PhotoVault (such as your PhotoVault media directory).
    Note: This scope does NOT allow PhotoVault to read, modify, or list your unrelated personal files, docs, or spreadsheets in Google Drive.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata (Application Data Storage):
    Allows PhotoVault to store encrypted configuration and vault recovery manifests in your dedicated Google Drive hidden app data folder so you can seamlessly recover your vault state upon device migration or reinstallation.

4. Information Handled by PhotoVault

All data processed by PhotoVault falls strictly into the following categories:

  • Media Content (Photos & Videos): Files you explicitly pick from your local device storage or camera to add to your PhotoVault. These files are processed locally (thumbnail extraction, checksum calculation) and uploaded directly to your connected Google Drive storage.
  • Google Account Identity: When you connect a Google account, we receive the account's email address and profile ID purely to distinguish between your connected storage accounts in the multi-account storage pool UI.
  • OAuth Authentication Tokens: Cryptographic access tokens and refresh tokens granted by Google OAuth 2.0 during sign-in, used exclusively to authenticate API requests directly to Google Drive.
  • Vault Metadata: Media metadata (file dimensions, MIME types, creation timestamps, SHA-256 hashes, remote file IDs, and album associations) stored in a local SQLite (Room) database on your device.

5. Data Storage & Security Practices

We implement industry-standard cryptographic and security controls to safeguard your information:

Hardware-Backed Key Protection

All OAuth tokens and sensitive credentials are encrypted using Android's EncryptedSharedPreferences, backed by master keys secured in the hardware-isolated Android Keystore.

  • Direct Encrypted Transmission: All network requests to Google Drive APIs use TLS 1.3 encryption.
  • Local Isolation: The local catalog database and thumbnail caches are stored within your application's private internal sandbox on Android, inaccessible to other standard apps.
  • No Telemetry or Analytic SDKs: PhotoVault contains zero tracking pixels, crash telemetry beacons that harvest personal data, or third-party analytics SDKs.

6. Third-Party Data Sharing Policy

We do not share, sell, rent, monetize, or disclose your personal information or media to any third party under any circumstances.

The only third-party service communicated with is Google APIs (Google Drive), solely for the purpose of fulfilling your explicit backup and retrieval requests.

7. Data Retention, Revocation & Deletion

You have absolute control over your data at all times:

  • Disconnecting Accounts: You can disconnect any linked Google account in PhotoVault under the Accounts settings. Disconnecting immediately removes and clears all locally stored OAuth tokens, cached credentials, and account metadata.
  • Deleting Media: When you delete media within PhotoVault, you can choose to delete the local copy, move the remote file in Google Drive to the Trash, or delete both.
  • Revoking Google Access: You can revoke PhotoVault's access at any time through your Google Account Security Dashboard (Google Third-Party Access Settings).
  • App Uninstallation: Uninstalling PhotoVault completely purges all local databases, cached thumbnails, and stored settings from your device. Your remote photos remain safe in your personal Google Drive account.

8. Children's Privacy

PhotoVault does not knowingly collect or solicit personal information from children under the age of 13. The application is a personal utility app intended for use with the user's own cloud storage accounts.

9. Updates to This Privacy Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time to reflect changes in our practices or regulatory requirements. Any updates will be published to this page with an updated "Effective Date".
